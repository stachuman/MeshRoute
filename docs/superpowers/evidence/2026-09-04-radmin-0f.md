## 3. ⛔ STOP — condition 2, with its measurement

> **STOP 2:** *"the canonical normal-send producer grows beyond 272 before NUL, **or another non-debug product
> producer's canonical line exceeds the derived `send` line**"*

**It fires on the second clause.** `send_layer` is:

- **a product verb, not a diagnostic** — it is in the operator `help` output beside `send` and `send_channel`
  (`src/firmware_commands.cpp:1009`), it is exercised by the maintained bench script
  (`docs/2026-07-31-bench-test-script.md:175`), and it carries two standing register rows (B60 OPEN/SPEC READY, B16 PARKED/SIM PARITY). It is not a

  every environment;
- **reachable over BLE** — `ble_dispatch_line` routes it through `parse_command` (`src/fw_main.cpp:543`) exactly
  as it routes `send`;
- **longer than `send`** — 274 bytes at the largest body it can deliver, 287 bytes at the same

### 3.1 The ruling itself names the verb the brief's derivation omitted

R-RA-24′ bound 2, as recorded at
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-03-remote-admin-v2-rulings.md:404-405` (the QA position the owner ratified),
states the requirement as:

> *"BLE `g_line` 160 → **270**, DERIVED as the longest syntactically legal local line + NUL, **so that every
> legal `send`/`send_channel`/`send_layer` line and every legal `remote` line fit**."*

The **derivation** is the ruling; the number that has accompanied it has now been corrected once already
(269/270 → 272/273, when `-K` was found). This is the same class of correction, one verb further out: the
269→272 pass enumerated `send`'s flags but not `send_layer`'s line, even though the ruling's own sentence
names `send_layer`.

### 3.2 A second, independent measurement — the brief contradicts itself even *within* `send`

This does not need `send_layer` at all. The brief's 0f-3 requires:

> *"a 240-byte body is preserved by parsing as far as the existing parser permits and is refused by
> `Node::on_command` as `err_too_large` — the Node's 239-byte DM authority, **not a newly invented parser
> limit**"*

and its Required result simultaneously requires:

> *"a 273-byte line dispatches nothing, emits exactly `{"err":"line_too_long"}\n`"*

But `send 0xffffffff "<240-byte body>" -a -e -t -K -l` **is exactly 273 bytes** — measured above
(`len=273, err=ok, body_len=240`). With a 273-byte buffer (272 accepted bytes) that exact line is refused by
the **transport** over BLE, not by `Node::on_command`. The 0f-3 native test would still pass, because native
has no BLE — which is precisely the "a transport overflow impersonating a semantic refusal" outcome the brief's
own layer-ownership table exists to prevent. The two requirements cannot both hold at 273.

### 3.3 Why this was not resolved by choosing 275 or 288 and continuing

1. The brief is the slice authority and its **Required result** hard-pins "272 accepted / 273 refused", its
   0f-1 requires the assertion that storage is **exactly** maximum-plus-NUL, and its Part 61 bench draft is
   written around those two numbers. Landing 275 or 288 would silently invalidate the brief's acceptance
   criteria, the bench part, and the number recorded in a ratified owner ruling.
2. The RAM figure the owner may care about moves with the choice (+113 / +115 / +128 B on nRF52), and
   R-RA-24 explicitly reserves that call: *"If the measured static-RAM cost of that derived buffer is
   unacceptable, the slice STOPS for a new owner ruling instead of shortening the command, truncating it, or
   silently accepting a smaller BLE limit."*
3. The choice between Option A and Option B is a **policy** question about which layer must own an over-long
   line's refusal — the same question the brief's own layer-ownership table settles for `send`. That is an
   Author/QA/owner ruling, not a coder's pick.

### 3.4 Recommendation handed to the Author (not implemented)

**Option A (288)** is the methodologically consistent one, for the reason the brief itself gives for `send`:
the brief admits a 239-byte `send` body specifically so that the sealed form reaches
`SealOutcome::too_large` by name instead of being dropped by the transport (its STOP 7). The identical
situation exists for `send_layer`: a 239-byte body is **admitted** by `node.cpp:2085` and then refused **by
name** — `MR_EMIT("xl_send_too_large", …)` + `CmdCode::err_too_large` at `lib/core/node_mac.cpp:786-787`.
Under Option B (275) that named refusal becomes unreachable over BLE and the operator sees `line_too_long`
instead, which is the exact failure mode STOP 7 forbids one verb over.

Whichever is ruled, the derived expression should keep `send_layer` as a first-class named term, so that a
future change to `gw_env_max_hops`, to the XL flag set, or to the accepted flag list moves the buffer
automatically — the property a bare literal would freeze, which is what happened here twice.
