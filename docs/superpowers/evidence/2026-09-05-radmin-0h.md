### (c) The one arm that can still overflow from an ADMITTED body — and why the pack check is not decorative

The delegated wrapper's **arm 2** (`itype != 0`, `node_hashlocate.cpp`) prefixes the wrapper body with a single
enclosed-TYPE byte. A user body **at** the 232 cap therefore builds a **233**-byte wrapper body and
`pack_unicast_inner` refuses (9 + 233 = 242 > 241). Measured:

| user body | wrapper body | inner | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 231 | 232 | **241** (exactly full) | `queued`, `SOURCE_HASH` set |
| 232 | 233 | — | **`dm_inner_too_large` exactly once**, `ctr` RETURNED, **0** queue items, no `tx_enqueue`, no `data_pack_failed`, `send_failed{too_large}` pushed |

⚠ **This is a genuine behaviour change at that arm, stated rather than buried:** before 0h the same input queued a
carrier with `SOURCE_HASH` stripped, which is precisely the [[B296]] shape-2 defect (the home then inboxed it). The
refusal is the ruled outcome, and the corpus census shows the arm is never exercised there (max wrapper body 200).

The **lifecycle gate** is measured on both sides, driven directly because no production caller can reach the negative
half (every carrier that reaches this arm — `MOBILE_SEND`, `0x00` — owns the generic lifecycle):
`type 0x00` → emit **and** push; `DATA_TYPE_MOBILE_KEY_FORWARD` (internal, `generic_send_lifecycle == false`) → emit,
**no push**. `CHECK(pushed == data_type_traits(ty).generic_send_lifecycle)`. STOP 4 does not fire.

### (d) Unknown-by-ID destination — the R-RA-25 exemption, and its price

`send <id>` to an id with **no** binding: at 232 the carrier sets `SOURCE_HASH` and **omits `DST_HASH`** (nothing was
invented), inner = 1 + 4 + 232 = **237**, i.e. deliberately SHORT of 241. At 233 it refuses exactly as a carrier that
does spend the four bytes — the unspent field buys **no extra capacity**, which is the ruling's second half asserted
as a fact rather than assumed.

### (e) Park backstops — refuse, never clamp

Reached DIRECTLY (friend), because `on_command` refuses over-cap input one layer above and would hide the property:

| helper | at 232 | at 233 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `park_send` (returns `bool`) | parks; stored `body_len == 232` | **`false`**, `send_park_refused` emitted, park count unchanged, slot 0 untouched |
| `park_send_layer` (`void`) | parks, `cross_layer` set, stored `body_len == 232` | **no second slot**, `send_layer_park_refused` emitted, slot 0 untouched |

A truncating clamp would have produced a second slot holding exactly `cap` bytes of an over-cap message; both readings
forbid it. Signatures and the `node.h` fence are untouched (STOP 10 does not fire).
